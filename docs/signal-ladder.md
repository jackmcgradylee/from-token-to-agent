# The Learning Signal Ladder

> A personal reading of the spine of the 16-week course, made concrete
> with the W1 tokenizer experiment.

## The 7 rungs

```text
labels
↓
word structure
↓
next-token
↓
preferences
↓
verifiers
↓
environment
↓
self-judge
```

This is the bottom of Prof. Tang's 2026-09-17 AML PPT. Each rung says
**where the supervised signal comes from**. From top to bottom, the
signal becomes more abundant and more automated, and humans exit one
more loop.

## Concrete walkthrough: from rung 1 to rung 2 in one evening

The W1 experiment
([`experiments/w01/exp-001-char-word-bpe-comparison/`](../experiments/w01/exp-001-char-word-bpe-comparison/))
made rung 2 ("word structure") tangible. We trained three tokenizers
on the same tiny corpus and measured them on held-out sentences with
rare English words, rare CJK characters, and emoji.

| Tokenizer | Where does its signal come from? | Vocab | UNK on eval | Bytes/token |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Char (byte-level) | The byte alphabet itself — 256 UTF-8 bytes. | 260 (fixed) | 0 | 1.00 |
| Word | A human-curated word list (built from the training corpus). | 107 | **38 (43%)** | 3.06 |
| BPE (byte-level) | Iterative merges of frequent byte pairs in the corpus. | 306 | 0 | 1.14 |

### What we see in the numbers

- **Char** has zero UNK by construction. **BPE** inherits that property
  because it shares the same 256-byte base. **Word** fails on the eval
  set because every out-of-vocabulary token collapses to `<unk>` —
  the entire rare-CJK line `嵚崟嶷魑魅魍魉孀嫠。` produces 9 UNKs.
- **Word** is the most "compressed" (3.06 bytes/token), but the
  compression comes at the price of losing anything the vocabulary
  hasn't seen. The information-theoretic view: word tokenizers
  trade *representational efficiency* for *coverage brittleness*.
- **Char** has zero compression (1.0 bytes/token). Every byte is its
  own token. The information is preserved bit-for-bit, but sequence
  lengths explode: a single CJK character costs 3 tokens, an English
  word of 5 letters costs 5 tokens.
- **BPE** splits the difference. It learns the most frequent byte
  pairs and merges them into single tokens. Frequent English words
  collapse to one token each; frequent CJK characters collapse to
  one token each (because each char is 3 fixed bytes that merge
  once seen often enough). The vocabulary is small enough to be
  tractable, but every UTF-8 string is still representable.

## How the ladder extends

### Rung 1 — labels (supervised)

Traditional ML: a human annotator writes "this image is a cat", and
the model learns `image -> {cat}`. Every example costs a human's time.

### Rung 2 — word structure (self-supervised at the input level)

Even before predicting the next token, we have to decide **what a
token is**. The W1 experiment shows this isn't free: word tokenization
dies on OOV, char tokenization explodes sequence length, and BPE sits
in between. The signal here is "the structure of language itself" —
no labels needed, but lots of text.

### Rung 3 — next-token prediction (W1-W7)

Once tokenization is fixed, the model's actual learning task is simple
— predict the next token given the previous ones. The signal is
**the text itself**. No labels. This is the unsupervised miracle that
made the modern LLM era possible.

### Rung 4 — preferences (W8-W10)

Once you have a base model, humans can rank its outputs: "this
response is better than that one." DPO turns those pairwise rankings
into a training signal. The signal is still human labour, but
*comparisons* are cheaper than *completions*.

### Rung 5 — verifiers (W10-W11)

For tasks with a programmatic check (math has exact answers; code has
unit tests), the verifier replaces the human. RLVR turns verifier
output into a reward; GRPO turns the reward into a policy update.
The signal is now machine-checkable, free of human bias.

### Rung 6 — environment (W12-W13)

The verifier becomes a running process. The model takes an action; the
environment (a shell, a repo, a browser) returns an observation;
the loop runs many turns. The signal is the trajectory outcome, not
a single-step reward.

### Rung 7 — self-judge (W14-W16)

The model itself becomes the judge. Successful trajectories (judged
by the environment's verifier) are distilled into the policy.
Failed trajectories are diagnosed and revised. The signal is now
*generated by the same system that consumes it*. Humans have exited
the feedback loop.

## What this means for the course

The 16 weeks aren't really about Transformer architectures, BPE
tokenizers, Triton kernels, or GRPO. They are about **building each
rung of this ladder with your own hands**, so that by the end you
can articulate *why* the rung is at that height, *what* would break
if you lowered it, and *how* you would raise the next one.

The W1 experiment is a 50-line proof of concept for rung 2. By W16,
we should be able to build a rung-7 self-improving loop in 200 lines
and explain every choice in it.
